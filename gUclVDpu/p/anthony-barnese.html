Their story

He registered for the draft on 30 June 1942, eighteen years old, giving his address as 620 North Fifth and his employer as Sam Barnese Grocery, 1000 Sixth Street — his father's store. The registrar put him down at five foot eight, 154 pounds, brown eyes, brown hair, ruddy. He signed it Tony Barnese.

On 16 February 1943 the Saginaw News printed the county's largest draft contingent to date and listed him among the men leaving Wednesday. That Wednesday was the seventeenth, and 17 February 1943 is the enlistment date on his service record — the paper announcing him gone and the army recording him arrived, one day apart. He served until November 1945 and was wounded; notices ran at home in 1944 and again in 1945. An Air Transport Command passenger list carries Pfc Anthony Barnese, serial 36569467, aged twenty, flying home.

By April 1950 he was in Los Angeles, twenty-five and single, living with Joseph and three other siblings. In January 1951 he stood as best man at Arthur's wedding in Tarzana; that October he was back in Saginaw marrying Alvina Krawczak.

In November 1963 he and Frank, with a partner named Dominic Distaso, tried to get card-room gambling legalised — first in Walnut, then in Pico Rivera, where the council rejected the ordinance unanimously on the night of 18 November. Frank would get his licence five years later in Cabazon. Anthony died on the last day of 2010, and Alvina followed in 2013.
