Their story

The eldest of Roman and Ramona's children. His 1942 draft card is unmistakably his: it names his mother, Ramona G. Sanchez of San Diego, as the person who would always know his address. It also shows he had made his own way — at 21 he was a student at the University of San Francisco, a long climb for a boy whose younger sisters had been placed in an orphanage after their parents' divorce. He was a big man: six foot two and a half, 235 pounds, red hair, brown eyes, a ruddy complexion, and a scar on his right wrist — carrying his mother Ramona's remarkable height into the next generation. From that University of San Francisco classroom he went into the Marine Corps as an officer, serving in World War II in the Pacific: in the spring of 1945 he moved up through Saipan, and on 5 June 1945 went ashore at Iheya Shima — a small island off Okinawa that the Marines seized in the final weeks of the Okinawa campaign to build airfields for the planned invasion of Japan. A second lieutenant then, he would rise to Captain — the commission his education had opened the door to. He lived to 1992. His headstone reads simply: John C. Sanchez, Capt., U.S. Marine Corps, World War II.
