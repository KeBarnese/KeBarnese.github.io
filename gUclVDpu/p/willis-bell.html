Their story

Barbara's second son, born at Evansville two days after Christmas in 1912, and four years old when she died. He signed himself Willis Henry Bell — which matters, because his brother Thomas's obituary calls him William, and that error has followed him since. His own hand, his Indiana birth record and his headstone all say Willis.

In October 1940 he was twenty-seven, working at the J. D. Blowney service station on North Saginaw Street in Flint, and living at 515 West Flint Park Boulevard. On the line asking for someone who would always know his address he wrote his brother Thomas Edward Bell — at the same address. Barbara's two sons had left Indiana for Michigan and were sharing a house.

Three months later he went into the Army, and stayed for ten and a half years, through one war and into another, coming out in August 1951. Afterwards he joined the Genesee County Sheriff's Department and retired from it — the department his brother would be elected to run in 1960.

He married Reubena Kietzman and raised her two children; no children of his own appear anywhere in the record. He died at Flint Osteopathic Hospital on 8 February 1981, aged sixty-eight, and was buried near Thomas at Evergreen.
