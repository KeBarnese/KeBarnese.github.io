Barnese · Barnesi (Sicily)

Carmela Covelli

1890 – 1970
Great-grandmother
Older generations — Now documented, not just inferred. Her 1908 marriage act and the guardianship-council papers that preceded it name her parents as Francesco Covelli and Francesca Gugliuzza — both already dead by 1908, which is why an orphaned Carmela needed a family council to authorize her marriage. Her guardian was her own grandmother, Rosalia Siracusa (daughter of Giovacchino Siracusa). Her brother Dominick's 1884 birth act now names Francesca's parents outright — Luigi Gugliuzza and Rosalia Siracusa — so the maternal line runs Carmela ← Francesca Gugliuzza ← Luigi Gugliuzza & Rosalia Siracusa ← Giovacchino Siracusa. (An earlier guess that the Giuseppe Gugliuzza on the 1908 council was a maternal uncle no longer holds, since Francesca's father was Luigi, not his father Rosario — a cousin at most.) The naming-custom guesses held up: her first son Joseph pointed to Salvatore's father Giuseppe, and her second son Frank to her own father Francesco. Her brother Dominick Covelli's WWI draft card names Lascari as his birthplace, confirming the Covelli as a Lascari family. Her own Lascari birth act has now turned up too (No. 53, Tribunale di Termini Imerese): it fixes her birth at 10 July 1890 — a day earlier than the 11 July the family had long used — and names her parents outright, Francesco Covelli and Francesca Gugliuzza. The birth was reported by the town midwife, Rosaria Miceli, and since the record calls Francesca the wife (not the widow) of Francesco, her father was still living in 1890; both parents would be gone by her 1908 marriage.

Their story

Crossed the Atlantic with her young daughter to join Salvatore, who had gone ahead, bound for Saginaw, Michigan. A few kilometers separated the Barnesi of Lascari from the Muffoletto of Campofelice back in Sicily. On the name: earlier U.S. records had her as Camilla Curella, but her own ship manifest reads Carmela Cuvelli, and two of her children — daughter Mary and son Art (on his marriage license) — independently listed her as Carmela Covelli. The manifest plus two children settle it: Carmela Covelli is the true name, and 'Camilla Curella' was a garbled transcription. On that same manifest the toddler who became Mary was written 'Salvatora Barnesi' — the original spelling, which would pass through Barnes before settling as Barnese. And she did not arrive to strangers: her brother Dominick Covelli had also come to America. His wife, Pasqua Auppa — Esther, in America — followed in April 1913 with their five-year-old son Francesco, bound for Chicago; a second son, Angelo — Carmela's nephew — died in Saginaw in 1915, not yet three years old, of malnutrition, a quiet measure of how hard those first immigrant years could be. The Covelli put down roots in Gary, Indiana — the same steel city as the Muffoletto family, whom Carmela's son Frank would one day marry into.
