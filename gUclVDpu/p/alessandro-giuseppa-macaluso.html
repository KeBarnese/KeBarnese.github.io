Their story

Rose's parents, of Collesano. Alessandro Macaluso crossed to America first and was living in Chicago — the card gives 377 South Clark Street — by 1910, when his wife Giuseppa Luisi, a seamstress, followed on the Duca d'Aosta with their three children, Dominica, Agostino, and eight-year-old Rosa, to join him. The family put down roots in the Chicago–Gary steel belt; an immigration cross-reference card shows Giuseppa still there in 1949, collecting a certificate of arrival at Hammond, Indiana. Her brother, Giuseppe Luisi, stayed behind in Collesano. Alessandro — born about 1871 — kept ties to the old country: a 1925 manifest catches him sailing back into Chicago from Naples that August, after a visit to Italy where his son Agostino was then living in Lascari. It's a small world of Madonie towns this whole family came from — Collesano, Campofelice, and Lascari all sit within a few miles of one another on the Palermo coast. Giuseppa outlived Alessandro; her own death notice ran in the Chicago Tribune on 20 Jan 1956, under the Americanized name "Rachel Macaluso, nee Louise" — likely Giuseppa Luisi rendered for an English-speaking readership, her maiden surname Luisi becoming "Louise." It names all three of her children by their married identities: Mamie (Peter) Dolce, Gus, and Rose (Sam) Muffoletto — the last an exact match for Rose Macaluso's own marriage to Sam Muffoletto — and counts 11 grandchildren and 13 great-grandchildren by 1956. She was waked at a chapel on South Sacramento Boulevard, sent off from Presentation Church, and buried at Mount Carmel Cemetery.
