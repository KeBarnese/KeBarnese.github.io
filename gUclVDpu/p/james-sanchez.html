Their story

Roman and Ramona's second son, and the one who walked the roughest road. After the family came apart he was living in Mexico — his own draft card notes he 'was residing in Mexico until 10-12-42' — before turning up in San Diego at nineteen and registering for the war, his mother Ramona named as next-of-kin. He served in the U.S. Navy: an Aviation Machinist's Mate in the aviation unit of the light cruiser USS Portsmouth (CL-102), tending her scout floatplanes, joining the crew in July 1945 and rising to Seaman First Class. Tall like the rest — six foot one and a half, gray eyes, brown hair, a scar across the bridge of his nose. In later life he served a long stretch in prison, the hardest chapter in this family's story. But his Navy service held: he was buried with military honors at San Joaquin Valley National Cemetery — the same ground as his brother John, the Marine captain. Two brothers, two very different lives, at rest in the same earth. He died on 7 December 2001 — the sixtieth anniversary, to the day, of the Pearl Harbor attack that had drawn his generation into the war. His family chose an epitaph that reaches past every hard chapter and lands on who he was underneath: 'Seeker of the Truth. Forever in our hearts.'
