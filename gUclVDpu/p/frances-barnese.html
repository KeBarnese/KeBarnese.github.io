Their story

She grew up in a grocery store and married into another one. Frances was born in Saginaw in April 1915, the third of Sam and Carmela's ten children, and was still living at 620 North Fifth at thirty-one when she married Frank J. Jese on 27 April 1946. He was a meat cutter then, the son of a Frank Jese already dead and of Frances Erzen; by the 1950 census he owned a grocery store of his own, and Frances was working its register as cashier. Their witnesses had been his brother and hers.

The Jese name behaves like the Barnese one. Frank's own obituary calls him “brother of Emil C. Jesse and Fred Stancel” — three surnames across one set of brothers, which points to a remarriage or a step-relationship somewhere behind their mother.

The family left Michigan. In April 1979 a Moab, Utah paper noted that “Mr. and Mrs. Frank Jese” had been visited by “2 sisters, Helen Barnese and Tina Barnese, from California” — in fact her sisters-in-law, Joseph's wife and Frank's, come to see her. That one paragraph in a small Utah weekly is the only surviving trace of the Jeses in Moab.

They moved on to Sacramento about 1989. Frank died there in September 1994, aged seventy-eight, a member of the Elk Grove Senior Center. Frances outlived him by eleven years and died on 6 March 2006, aged ninety.
