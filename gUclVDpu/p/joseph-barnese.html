Their story

Sam and Carmela's eldest son, born in Saginaw in 1913. At twenty-two he was already running a grocery of his own at 1707 Farwell Street, and in August 1935 he married Helen Szukala — a Saginaw girl then living in Detroit, daughter of Peter Szukala and Katherine Wisniewska. They were married in Detroit at Our Lady Help of Christians by a Polish priest, with her sister and a Saginaw friend as witnesses: a Sicilian grocer's son marrying into the Polish east side, which is what nearly all of his brothers and sisters would do.

His 1940 draft card is a small portrait of a man of twenty-seven: 1626 Syracuse Street, telephone 9388, five foot six, 145 pounds, brown eyes and brown hair, employer entered simply as “has grocery store of his own.” The person who would always know his address was his father, at 620 North Fifth. He signed it Joe Barnese.

Then he went west, and took half the family with him. By the 1950 census he was living in Los Angeles as the proprietor of a bowling establishment — and under his roof were four of his siblings: Louis, tending bar at the alley and dead within a year; Anthony, home from the war; Elizabeth, clerking for an advertising firm; and eighteen-year-old Samuel Jr. That one census sheet is the clearest surviving record of the family's move to California.

Bowling became the family trade, and Joseph started it. Frank opened the Pico-Rivera Bowl in 1955; Joe and his son Sam were at Hollywood Lanes by 1960, and Sam went on to own Hollywood Star Lanes for more than forty years; Joe was still in the business at Newhall in 1980. He died in Los Angeles in 1986, aged seventy-three, having last lived in Tarzana.
