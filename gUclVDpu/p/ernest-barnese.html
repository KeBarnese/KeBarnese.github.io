Their story

He lived eleven years and one month, all of them at 620 North Fifth, and he died there on 10 September 1943 after what the paper called a lingering illness. He was the tenth and last of Sam and Carmela's children.

His obituary in the Saginaw News is the keystone of this whole branch. Until it surfaced, only six of the ten children were known. In eleven lines it names every one of them: “six brothers, Frank, Louis and Anthony in the army, Joseph, Arthur and Junior, all of Saginaw; three sisters, Mrs. Anthony Karaba, Frances and Betty Jane, all of Saginaw.” Arthur, Samuel Jr. and Betty Jane exist on this site because an eleven-year-old's death notice listed them.

The date matters too. He is why we know Frank, Louis and Anthony were all in the army in September 1943, and his family buried him while three of his brothers were away at the war.

He was buried at Mt. Olivet. Friends were welcome to see him at the home from eleven o'clock on the Sunday, and the funeral was at nine on the Tuesday morning.
