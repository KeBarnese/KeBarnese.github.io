Their story

The least documented of the ten, and the one who died youngest of those who reached adulthood. Louis was born in Saginaw about 1921, and in September 1943 his little brother Ernest's obituary listed him among the brothers “in the army” alongside Frank and Anthony.

By April 1950 he was in Los Angeles, twenty-nine and unmarried, living in Joseph's household and working as a bartender at his brother's bowling alley. He was dead within the year.

Something about his death drew unusual notice. Obituaries survive in at least four papers — the Saginaw News back home, and three in California, including The Tidings, the Los Angeles archdiocesan weekly. Four obituaries for a bartender of thirty is not ordinary, and none of them has yet been read. There is also a set of 1947 clippings in the family file describing a false confession he is said to have made, and a follow-up reporting it unfounded. What that episode was, and whether it bears on his death four years later, is entirely unknown.

This page is deliberately thin. Everything above rests on a census line and an eleven-year-old's obituary. The documents that would fill it in are already gathered and simply unread.
