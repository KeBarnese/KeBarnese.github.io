Hunter (Kentucky)

William Henry Hunter

1878 – 1966
2× great-grandfather
Older generations — Older Hunter generations go here once documented.
Born
11 Jan 1878 · Webster County, Kentucky map from his death certificate and his 1942 draft card, which agree. His 1918 card gives 11 Jan 1877 — a year out
Died
31 Jan 1966, 5 a.m. · Our Lady of Mercy Hospital, Morganfield, Union County, aged 88 map of atelectasis following pneumonia, with arteriosclerosis and cardiac failure underlying; his doctor had attended him since September 1965. Usual residence N. Highway 60, Morganfield. The certificate records him as widowed, so Ida died before him. His son Austin Hunter was the informant
Married
married Ida E. Thomas on 13 Mar 1900 at J. M. Hunter's residence, Union County bond of one hundred dollars at Morganfield the same day, G. H. Sutton as surety; rites by Alfred Cullen, J.P., before Gus Greenwell and Jas. M. Hunter. Both recorded as first marriages. She died on 2 Dec 1956, which is why his own certificate records him as widowed — he outlived her by nine years and two months
Parents
James Hunter & ?, both born in Webster County, Kentucky the 1900 marriage bond gives the birthplace of both his parents as Webster County; his 1966 death certificate names his father as James Hunter and his mother as unknown. The wedding was held at “J. M. Hunter's residence,” and a Jas. M. Hunter witnessed it, while J. M. and C. E. Hunter attested Ida's consent — and the 1910 census puts James M. Hunter, 50, and Clara E., 50 in the dwelling immediately next door. That is very likely his father and stepmother. The age is tight — James M. would have been about eighteen when William was born — so it is not yet proven
Siblings
?
Children
Valeria A. (1901), Lola M. (1902), Willis Laurie (1904), James Thomas (1906), Flo Ella Mary (1909), Austin E. (1911), Goldie K. (1915) and Della E. (1917) full names and ages from the 1920 census, which lists all eight at home. “Aunt Willie” of the family memoir was Willis Laurie, a daughter; “Jim Tom” was James Thomas. But the 1910 census records Ida as mother of seven children, six living — and only six were then born of the eight. So a ninth child died before 1910 and is named nowhere
Occupation
Farmer — general farming in Union County from 1900 to the 1940s; retired by 1950 in 1918 farming for Mrs. Henrietta Cavier at Boxville; on his own account at Waverly by 1942. Eight grades of school

Their story

Head of a large Union County family — eight children in all — living in Caseyville by 1910. A good, sturdy Kentucky name.
