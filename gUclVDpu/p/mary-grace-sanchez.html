Their story

After her parents' divorce, young Grace and her sister Cecilia were placed with the nuns at Nazareth Home orphanage in San Diego. She hated it, and said the nuns were cruel. By April 1950 she was in Los Angeles, eighteen years old and boarding as a cousin in the household of Manuel Guerrero — a 29-year-old electrician's apprentice — along with his siblings Anthony, Theresa, and Benjamin, and his married sister Dolores, by then Dolores Pena, with her husband George and their two small children. Nine people in one house, and Grace listed simply as "cousin," working forty hours a week tagging clothes at a cleaning establishment. The enumerator wrote her name as "Tracie," but the hand is unmistakably "Gracie" once set beside the same capital G elsewhere on the page. Manuel's own WWII draft card, filled out eight years earlier in 1942, names "Dolores Guerrero, 327 North Kern Ave., Los Angeles" as the person who would always know his address — the same house number the 1950 census gave without a street name. That Dolores turns out to be his mother, not his sister: her own naturalization petition identifies her as Dolores (Guzman) Guerrero, born 1887 in Mazatlán — and Ramona's page now carries her as Ramona's own sister, eleven years older. So the household that took Grace in wasn't a loose cousin connection; it was her mother's sister's children. Manuel, Anthony, and the younger Dolores (Pena) were Grace's actual first cousins, and 327 N. Kern Ave. was her aunt's house before it was theirs — hers until at least 1954, when Grace married and left it. Her brother James was there too that spring, though the census missed him: a Los Angeles Times robbery report from 6 May 1950, a month after the census, gives his address as the same 327 N. Kern Ave., and his cousin Manuel's as well — the arrest that sent James to San Quentin (see his page). The house itself was built in 1942 — the same year as Manuel's draft card — meaning it was new when the family took it and was still standing, and still theirs, when the 1950 census found Grace there. She carries the family's Indigenous maternal line — her DNA showed mitochondrial haplogroup B2, tracing straight back through Ramona and Maria Lopez. She married Jack Bell in 1954 and built a family of her own. Like her brother John, she had red hair — a trait that most likely came down from their father Roman's Asturian roots in northern Spain, where red hair runs far more common than in the Mediterranean south. So she carried two inheritances at once: an Indigenous Mexican maternal line, and a head of northern-Spanish red hair.

— enumerated 5 Apr 1950 by Trinidad L. Aragon. House no. 327 (street name below, from Manuel's draft card). Household: Manuel Guerrero (head, 29, electrician's apprentice), Anthony (brother, 27, steel worker), Theresa (sister, 23), Benjamin (brother, 20, furniture maker), George Pena (brother-in-law, 27, auto parts) & Dolores (sister, 25) with children Georgie (5) and Linda (4), and, continuing onto the next sheet, Sanchez, "Tracie" [Gracie] — cousin, 18, never married, born California, working forty hours a week tagging clothes at a cleaning establishment. Ages match their mother's 1944 naturalization petition exactly for Manuel, Antonio, and Dolores, but the census gives two siblings English names — "Theresa" and "Benjamin" — where the petition has Maria (b. 1927) and Jose (b. 1929) at the matching ages; likely everyday Anglicized names rather than an error, but unconfirmed
