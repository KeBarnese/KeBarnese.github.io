Their story

The ninth of the ten and the one who came back. At the 1950 census Samuel Jr. was eighteen and living in Los Angeles in Joseph's household with three older siblings — and his Social Security number, issued in California, confirms it. He did not stay.

In 1955 his father retired and handed him the grocery at Sixth and Kirk. The old building came down that summer in stages, rebuilt in two phases so the store lost barely a week of trade, and reopened in January 1959 as the Sam Barnese Super Market — sixty-four thousand dollars of concrete block and brick, with steaks at sixty-nine cents a pound and a page of door-prize winners.

He married Marjorie M. Dew on Saturday 16 July 1955 at Holy Family Church, and they made their home at 1002 North Sixth, across the street from the store. His nephew Sam Barnese came from Tarzana to be best man; his nephew Joseph Karaba came from Phoenix to usher; Arthur's wife Elena was a bridesmaid. Five hundred people came to the reception. The newspaper listed the bridegroom's parents as living at Tarzana, California.

That last detail is the point of him. Every other surviving brother and sister ended in California — Joseph at Tarzana, Frank at Whittier, Anthony at Palmdale, Betty at Santa Clarita, Arthur at Chatsworth, Frances at Sacramento, and their parents at Tarzana too. Samuel Jr. had been out there at eighteen and chose Michigan. He died at Gulliver, on Lake Michigan in the Upper Peninsula, in October 2007. The son who inherited the business is the only one who did not go west.
