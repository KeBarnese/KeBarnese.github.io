Salvatore (Sam) Barnesi

Great-grandfather
Older generations — His 1908 civil marriage act (Lascari) now confirms his parents outright: Giuseppe Barnisi and Carmela Barnizzi — vindicating the earlier guess from the Sicilian naming custom, since his first son was indeed named Joseph. One open thread: that same marriage act records Salvatore as born in Pachino (province of Syracuse, southeast Sicily), which does not square with the family's Lascari/Palermo associations or the 'Cesille' scrawled on his U.S. draft card. Their story

The Barnesi patriarch who left Lascari for America. He sailed alone, arriving 11 August 1910, when his daughter Mary was not yet a year old — he had been there for her birth the previous September, but would miss her toddler years. Carmela and the little girl did not cross to join him until July 1912, almost two years later, by which time she was nearly three. He left an infant and was reunited with a child. The family name traveled from Barnesi through Barnes — the clipped, Americanized form on his early census records — before settling as Barnese. He made a life on this side of the ocean: by the time his son Frank left for the war in 1940, Sam owned a grocery store. His own 1942 draft registration puts the store at 1000 N. 6th Street in Saginaw and records his birthplace — phonetically — as 'Cesille, Italy,' an unrecognized spelling almost certainly a clerk's rendering of his Sicilian home. He spent his last years in California, dying in Tarzana in 1971.
