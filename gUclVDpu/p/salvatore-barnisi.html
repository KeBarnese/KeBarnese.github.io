Salvatore (Sam) Barnesi

1888 – 1971
Great-grandfather
Older generations — His actual Pachino birth act (No. 232, 28 April 1888) now settles both questions. It confirms his parents outright — Giuseppe Barnisi, aged ~50, and Carmela — vindicating the earlier guess from the Sicilian naming custom, since his first son was indeed named Joseph. (The mother's maiden surname reads Barnizzi — the form the Pachino registrar himself used in 1908; the Lascari clerk, taking it down at second hand 250 km away, wrote Barnizri.) And it resolves the birthplace: he really was born in Pachino, in the far southeast of Sicily (province of Syracuse), some 250 km from the Lascari/Palermo towns of the Covelli and Muffoletto lines. How a Pachino man came to marry into and settle with a Lascari family is now the standing puzzle — but the birthplace itself is no longer in doubt.

Their story

The Barnesi patriarch who left Lascari for America. He sailed alone, arriving 11 August 1910, when his daughter Mary was not yet a year old — he had been there for her birth the previous September, but would miss her toddler years. Carmela and the little girl did not cross to join him until July 1912, almost two years later, by which time she was nearly three. He left an infant and was reunited with a child. The family name moved through four spellings, each of them on a real document: Barnisi (the Sicilian original, on his 1908 marriage act), Barnesi (his ship manifest and earliest American records), the clipped Barnes (on his early U.S. census records), and finally Barnese. None of that was the family deciding to respell itself. Salvatore's father was illiterate — his 1888 birth act notes he could not sign — so for most of these lives the name was written FOR them, by whichever clerk held the pen, who set down what he heard. In that world a surname was a sound, not a fixed string of letters. You can watch it happen in miniature in Salvatore's own mother's maiden name, recorded Barnizri on one act and Barnirzi on another, the r and z simply swapped; and in his birthplace, rendered 'Cesille' by a Michigan draft clerk who had never heard of Pachino. A single, stable spelling only arrived once the American-born generation could write the name for themselves. He made a life on this side of the ocean: by the time his son Frank left for the war in 1940, Sam owned a grocery store. His own 1942 draft registration puts the store at 1000 N. 6th Street in Saginaw and records his birthplace — phonetically — as 'Cesille, Italy,' an unrecognized spelling almost certainly a clerk's rendering of his Sicilian home. He spent his last years in California, dying in Tarzana in 1971.
