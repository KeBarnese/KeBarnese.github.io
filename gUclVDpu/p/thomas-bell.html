Their story

The eldest of Henry Bell's six sons, and the only one who became a public figure. He was born at Morganfield in Union County, Kentucky, in September 1909, and lost his mother to tuberculosis before he was eight.

He left home for Michigan about 1930, in the worst of the Depression, and did not come back for seven years. By 1940 he and his brother Willis were sharing a house at 515 West Flint Park Boulevard — Willis's draft card that October names Thomas, at the same address, as the person who would always know where to find him.

He made a career in Flint: Buick skilled trades and UAW Local 599, then township supervisor, then the county board, then twelve years as Sheriff of Genesee County, from 1960 to 1972. He was past president of the Michigan Sheriffs Association and had chaired the county Democratic Committee. Willis, who retired from the Genesee County Sheriff's Department, spent part of his own working life in the department his brother ran.

He died at Crystal River, Florida on 1 December 1979, aged seventy, and was buried at Evergreen in Grand Blanc. His obituary is the document that made the rest of this family findable — it is the only source that lists all six brothers together and separates the two mothers.
