### Order Giuseppa's 1940 Alien Registration form (AR-2)

**Alien Registration Number: A1044592**

The 1940–41 Alien Registration index returns three hits, all on the same
A-number — one person indexed under both married and maiden name:

| Indexed name | Document ID | Detail given |
|---|---|---|
| Giusepp Macaluso | M051b00477 | b. **27 Mar 1874**, arr. **26 May 1910** |
| Giuseppa Macaluso | M051b00477 | b. 1874, reg. Chicago, Cook Co., IL |
| Giuseppa Luisi | L046b00621 | b. 1874, reg. Chicago, Cook Co., IL |

The 26 May 1910 arrival date matches the *Duca d'Aosta*, which sailed Palermo
12 May 1910 — 14 days. That confirms the registration belongs to our Giuseppa
and removes the risk of paying for the wrong file.

- [ ] **Check first whether the AR-2 image is already digitized.** The Document
      ID numbers suggest scanned microfilm; the image may already be attached to
      the index entry. Don't pay if it's free.
- [ ] If not: file **Form G-1041A** (Genealogy Records Request) with USCIS.
      *Skip Form G-1041* — that's the index search, and its only purpose is to
      find the file number, which we already have.
      - File number: A1044592
      - Record type: Alien Registration Form (AR-2)
      - Verify the current fee and whether online filing now exists; the USCIS
        pages are inconsistent and the figures found were stale.
      - Born 1874, so she is past the 100-year presumption-of-death threshold —
        a death certificate should not be required. Confirm on the form
        instructions.
- [ ] The AR-2 carries a **photograph and signature**, plus address, occupation,
      employer, organizations, relatives here and abroad, and whether she had
      ever applied for citizenship. Add the photo to her page if it survives.

### Citizenship note worth putting on the page

Giuseppa was still registering as an alien in 1940 — roughly thirty years after
arriving. Until the **Cable Act of 22 Sept 1922**, a wife automatically derived
citizenship when her husband naturalized. Her alien status in 1940 therefore
implies **Alessandro did not naturalize between his 1916 denial and Sept 1922**.
After 1922 she would have had to petition independently regardless, which fits
the 1949 Certificate of Arrival at Hammond already on the page — she was still
trying at 75.

- [ ] Verify the Cable Act reasoning before publishing it as fact.

## Barnese

### Frank Barnese — El Cid Casino, Cabazon

Six clippings gathered, not yet added to the site. The site currently holds
`barnese-frank-cabazon-casino-1968.jpg` and `barnese-frank-cardpalace-1963.jpg`.

| Date | Paper | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 8 Jul 1968 | *Desert Sun* (Palm Springs) p.2 | "Poker Players Get 2nd Cabazon Casino." El Cid opened the previous week, licensed to **Frank Barnese**, Pico Rivera bowling alley operator, and **Edward Roban**, retired Covina fireman. Same building as the "Little Casino," a $250,000 hall that closed Dec 1958 after a fire, loss $590,400. |
| 5 Jul 1968 | *Daily Record-Gazette* (Banning) p.3 | Chief Cafe ad, "Welcome to Cabazon / El Cid Casino." |
| 25 Oct 1968 | *Record-Gazette* p.2 | "Cabazon Casino Not Sold." Councilman Thomas DeLuca on whether the $25,000 licence transfers. Owners deny selling. **Frank's surname misprinted "Barmise."** |
| 1 Nov 1968 | *San Bernardino County Sun* p.17 | Editorial, "Cabazon Embroiled in Political Turmoil." City of ~900 with two casinos. El Cid paid **$25,000** first-year licence, renewals **$12,500**; city already in deficit. DeLuca ousted as mayor 3–2. |
| 26 Aug 1970 | *Record-Gazette* p.1 | "Cabazon City Council To View 'Hoopo' Game." Refers to "**the old El Cid building**" — the only evidence the casino had closed. |

- [ ] Add the clippings to `records/vital/` under the existing naming
      convention and list them on Frank's page.
- [ ] **El Cid ran under two years** — c. June 1968 to before Aug 1970. Note on
      the page that the closure is an *inference* from the phrase "old El Cid
      building." No article reporting the closure has been found.
- [ ] Search *Record-Gazette*, **1969 to mid-1970**, for a licence revocation,
      bankruptcy notice or fire report.
- [ ] Likely cause worth stating carefully: a $25,000 first-year fee on a card
      room in a town of 900, split with a second house chasing the same I-10
      traffic. The Little Casino had already failed on the same site.
